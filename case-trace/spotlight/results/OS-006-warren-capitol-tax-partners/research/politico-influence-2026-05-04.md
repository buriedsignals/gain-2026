# A new class of super PACs is emerging. Voters are wary.

By [DANIEL BARNES](https://www.politico.com/staff/daniel-barnes) and [JACOB WENDLER](https://www.politico.com/staff/jacob-wendler)

05/04/2026 06:00 PM EDT

Presented by

[![](https://static.politico.com/96/d0/9d49f300434b9c21cd7f30de71c6/acslogo.png)](https://www.politico.com/newsletters/politico-influence/2026/05/04/a-new-class-of-super-pacs-is-emerging-voters-are-wary-00905570)

_With help from Daniel Lippman and Marcia Brown_

Pluralities of Americans say investing in cryptocurrency isn’t worth the risk and AI is developing too quickly, according to [new results from The POLITICO Poll](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/03/poll-ai-crypto-super-pacs-voter-skepticism-midterms-00903376), even as those industries are spending millions to back industry-friendly candidates in the midterms, our colleagues Erin Doherty, Jasper Goodman, Jessica Piper and Brendan Bordelon reported over the weekend with Daniel B.

— The broad public skepticism creates a possible conflict for candidates benefiting from an influx of contributions from crypto and AI groups.

— It’s too early to say how candidates associated with these groups will fare in November. But in hypothetical head-to-head matchups in the April survey conducted by independent firm **Public First**, poll respondents were much less likely to choose candidates backed by a campaign group seeking looser regulations on artificial intelligence than candidates backed by a group advocating for more stringent rules on AI and tech companies. Those polled were also more likely to support a group advocating for policies to protect the environment and prevent climate change.

— **Leading the Future**, a pro-AI super PAC founded in August, has already raised more than $75 million since its launch, according to recent filings with the Federal Election Commission. **Fairshake**, a pro-crypto group primarily funded by **Coinbase**, **Andreessen** **Horowitz** and **Ripple** **Labs**, is expected to back candidates in both parties and has [already spent $28 million across several competitive primaries](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/02/17/cyrpto-super-pac-illinois-primaries-00784504?utm_medium=twitter&utm_source=dlvr.it) through its network of PACs.

— Both industries are also spending big on Washington lobbyists to ensure their influence continues past Election Day. The AI lobby in particular has ballooned in recent years; **OpenAI** and **Anthropic** spent record amounts of money on lobbyists in the first quarter of 2026. The crypto industry has also poured millions into lobbying efforts in recent years to push Congress to enact a sweeping overhaul of how digital assets are regulated.

— “The universal thread, from their perspective, is, I think an attempt to maintain a degree of bipartisanship and identify people whom they think will be champions on these issues,” said **Jason** **Thielman**, former executive director of the **NRSC** and founding partner of public affairs firm **S2R**, of the crypto-aligned groups.

— The AI groups spending big in elections want to ensure their nascent industry is regulated by one set of federal rules, not a state-by-state patchwork, as state legislators rapidly [pass](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/10/04/sacramento-california-ai-rules-00594082) [new](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/12/19/kathy-hochul-signs-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-aimed-at-tech-industry-heavyweights-00700473) [laws](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/transparency-coalition-publishes-2025-state-ai-legislation-report) regulating the technology. The White House and congressional Republicans have generally supported that goal, but have so far floated light-touch regulations that most Democrats believe don’t go far enough.

— “There is a lot of work that needs to be done to help the voting public fully appreciate the national security threat that we face if we are not first in \[the AI\] race,” Thielman said of AI-affiliated groups. “It’s essential that \[the\] industry continue to invest very aggressively here, both to increasingly educate the public, educate policy makers because the issue is somewhat mixed from a public opinion perspective.”

**FIRST IN PI —** **VOGEL HEADS WEST: Vogel** **Group** is opening a new office in Arizona, its first outpost in the West, as the bipartisan lobbying firm expands its footprint outside the Beltway, Jacob reports. **Linley Wilson**, a veteran of Arizona GOP politics and a partner at sister law firm **Holtzman Vogel**, is coming on board to help the firm set up shop in the Grand Canyon State. The Arizona office will be Vogel Group’s fourth state and local affairs team, in addition to teams in Florida, Tennessee and Virginia.

— “Our clients’ challenges increasingly span Washington and the states, and we’ve been steadily building our platform to match that demand,” CEO **Alex Vogel** told PI. “Expanding into Arizona is the next step, with additional strategic moves ahead.” The firm counts chipmaker giant **Nvidia**, the **Plastics Industry Association** and social media platform **Bluesky** among its clients.

— Wilson spent nearly three years working in the Arizona House of Representatives, including as deputy chief of staff and general counsel for the Republican caucus. Before that, she spent more than a decade in the Arizona attorney general’s office, including leading the criminal appeals section across Phoenix and Tucson as criminal appeals chief counsel and deputy solicitor general.

**BEHIND END CITIZENS UNITED’S RETREAT: End Citizens United**, the liberal PAC focused on fighting big money in politics, is [no longer the political spending powerhouse](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/02/end-citizens-united-fundraising-spending-00903044) it once was, Elena Schneider and Jessica Piper report in a weekend deep-dive.

— The group was among the top 20 outside spenders in the 2016, 2018 and 2020 cycles, but its independent expenditures have since fallen off. The PAC spent just $1.7 million in the 2024 cycle — down from more than $14 million in 2020. And the group’s nonprofit arm that was supposed to fund a $9 million initiative to register young voters in six battleground districts across New York spent only $1 million total in 2024, according to IRS filings reviewed by POLITICO.

— The group says it’s shifted its emphasis to events, building relationships with candidates and elected officials, and engaging with volunteers. But former employees and Democratic strategists warn the group’s retreat from spending is contributing to its waning influence. Democrats struggled to accomplish the group’s main goal of enacting campaign finance reform during the Biden administration, and some lawmakers who took its pledge in 2018 not to accept corporate PAC money later did just that.

— End Citizens United isn’t the only organization struggling in the current ecosystem: Many Democratic groups have been grappling with higher costs associated with reaching donors and a more difficult fundraising landscape in recent years, Elena and Jessica write.

**WHERE’S THE BEEF:** A Justice Department antitrust investigation into the big four beef processors ( **JBS**, **Cargill**, **Tyson** **Foods** and **National** **Beef**) is proceeding, acting Attorney General **Todd Blanche** said in a news conference today as he detailed the department’s review of more than 3 million documents plus interviews with industry participants like ranchers, beef producers and processors.

— The investigation launched in November after [Trump directed then-AG Bondi](https://x.com/AGPamBondi/status/1986902658116956221) to look into alleged price fixing by beef industry heavyweights, Daniel B. writes. Trump’s initial announcement blamed the rising cost of beef on “majority foreign owned meat packers” which includes Brazilian-owned JBS and National Beef. White House trade adviser **Peter Navarro** echoed that message during today’s news conference.

— “I hasten to add here that the Brazilians are far more of the problem, and it’s complicated by the fact that the Brazilians, particularly JBS, hands out millions of dollars to our American political system like it’s candy,” Navarro said. “And the rate of return they get on that would make a Wall Street hedge fund blush, and we have got to put a stop to that.”

— Navarro also said the DOJ plans to settle an antitrust case against data company **Agri Stats**, which the DOJ had accused of enabling anticompetitive practices in the chicken, pork and turkey industries. That case is scheduled to go to trial later this month.

— “A weak settlement would only reinforce what we’ve seen too often in meatpacking price-fixing cases: corporations pay a fraction of what they gained, call it a cost of doing business, and keep the system rigged,” **Farm** **Action** President **Angela** **Huffman** said in a statement to our colleague Marcia Brown.

**ICYMI:** The White House has tapped **FGS Global**’s **Richard Walters** to join the West Wing as a deputy chief of staff, our colleague [Eli Stokols reported Friday](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/01/former-rnc-official-richard-walters-joining-white-house-for-midterm-push-00903228?utm_medium=twitter&utm_source=dlvr.it). Walters, an **RNC** alum who served as the executive director of Trump’s 2025 Inaugural Committee, is expected to take over some of the responsibilities previously managed by **James Blair**, who took a temporary leave of absence to focus on the president’s political operation ahead of the midterms.
