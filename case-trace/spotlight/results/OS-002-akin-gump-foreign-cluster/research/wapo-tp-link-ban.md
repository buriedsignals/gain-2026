# U.S. agencies back banning popular home WiFi device, citing national security risk

The Commerce Department has proposed barring sales of TP-Link products, citing a national security risk from ties to China, people familiar with the matter said.

Updated October 30, 2025

11 min

The Commerce Department led an interagency risk assessment of TP-Link home routers that concluded a ban is warranted for national security reasons. (Salwan Georges/The Washington Post)

![](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-apps/imrs.php?src=https://s3.amazonaws.com/arc-authors/washpost/4b90debd-fbbe-4e2b-a83c-78a9e1a9d347.png&h=196&w=196)

More than a half-dozen federal departments and agencies backed a proposal to ban future sales of the most popular home routers in the United States on the grounds that the vendor’s ties to mainland China make them a national security risk, according to people briefed on the matter and a communication reviewed by The Washington Post.
