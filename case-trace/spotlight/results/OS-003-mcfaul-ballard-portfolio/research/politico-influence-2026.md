**WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN: Donald Trump**’s second presidency has ushered in a massive payday for Washington’s top lobbying shops, delivering a revenue boom across K Street in exchange for help navigating the widespread disruption of the first year of Trump’s new term.

— **Ballard Partners**, which signed more than 200 new clients since the 2024 election, raked in $88.3 million in lobbying fees last year, a 350 percent increase compared to 2024, when the firm reported $19.6 million in lobbying revenues. Ballard reported six contracts during Q4 worth at least half a million dollars and collected six figures from clients including **Balkan Energy DMCC**, **Korea Zinc**, **Kimberly-Clark**, **Palantir** and **UnitedHealthcare**, disclosures show.

— K Street’s previous top earner, **Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck**, brought in $73.9 million in lobbying revenues in 2025, up from $67.9 million in 2024. **BGR Group** rounded out the top three with $71.5 million in revenues, which represents a 58 percent increase from 2024.

— “Every quarter it seems that there are more challenges and opportunities from the administration and Congress,” **Nadeam Elshami**, who co-leads Brownstein’s lobbying practice, told PI of the deluge of activity on K Street. Uncertainty around artificial intelligence continued to be a major driver of business in 2025, according to Elshami, who said that AI comes up on almost “every single call” with clients, who want advice on everything from how to remain competitive on AI to what the Trump administration will announce next.

— Tariffs were another huge issue on K Street last year. “It’s hard to think of a client that didn’t have questions related to tariffs, even if they weren’t exporters or importers, they were still impacted in one way or another,” said **Paul Stimers**, who co-leads the lobbying practice at **Holland & Knight**. Stimers’ firm brought in $54.6 million in lobbying revenues last year, up almost 10 percent from the prior year.

— Lobbyists expect trade to continue dominating Washington in 2026, especially as businesses await the Supreme Court’s decision on the legality of Trump’s so-called reciprocal tariffs. And even as they forecast a slowdown in legislation as the midterms approach, K Streeters said they’ll remain busy with oversight, defense, critical minerals, appropriations, transportation, national security and more — to say nothing of the Trump administration’s foreign policy moves up and down the Western Hemisphere.

— “Frankly, the lesson learned going forward is: Don’t be surprised,” Elshami said when asked about the top takeaways from the first year of Trump 2.0.

**THE TRUMP BUMP ON STEROIDS:** Of the top 20 earners overall last year, lobbying revenues were up by 10 percent or more at 13 of them compared to 2024, according to a PI analysis of disclosure filings. But while the windfall from Trump’s trade, tax, health care, AI, defense and energy policies boosted the bottom lines of virtually all of K Street’s biggest firms in 2025, the biggest jackpots were reserved for a select number of firms with close ties to Trump or his administration.

— A few of the top Trump-linked firms, including Ballard, **Miller Strategies**, **Mercury Public Affairs**, **CGCN** and **Michael Best Strategies**, saw even bigger boosts. Miller Strategies brought in $49.1 million, almost quadrupling its lobbying revenues from 2024. Mercury’s lobbying revenues nearly doubled to $22.3 million last year, while CGCN’s shot up by more than 82 percent to $17.6 million.

— Among the upstart firms who cashed in on Trump’s second term were **Continental Strategy**, which saw its lobbying revenues skyrocket from just $1.8 million in 2024 to more than $27 million in 2025. **Checkmate Government Relations**, which received $70,000 from just one client in 2024, reported a little over $21 million from more than 80 clients in 2025.

— Even so, Ballard Partners founder **Brian Ballard** pushed back today on the notion that his firm’s close ties to the Trump administration portend a short-lived success. “We remain doggedly committed to growing a fiercely bipartisan firm that is built to thrive in Washington’s dynamic political environment for decades to come,” he said in a statement on the shop’s 2025 revenues.

**FIRST IN PI:** More than two dozen conservative advocacy groups and think tanks are urging members of Congress to rebuff Trump’s [sudden push to cap credit card interest rates](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/01/09/trump-says-he-will-temporarily-cap-credit-card-rates-00721147) and rein in credit card swipe fees, arguing that “price controls harm consumers and have repeatedly failed to fix the very problem they attempt to solve.”

— “These proposals would reduce consumer credit access, fail to address affordability concerns, and fragment an already dependable, well-functioning payments system,” leaders from **Americans for Prosperity**, **Taxpayers Protection Alliance**, **Americans for Tax Reform**, the **Center for a Free Economy**, the **Competitive Enterprise Institute**, **R Street** and more [warn in a letter](https://subscriber.politicopro.com/f/?id=0000019b-e2d4-d452-a99b-ebfeb73c0000) that will be sent to lawmakers tomorrow morning.

— Top congressional Republicans [have already thrown cold water](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/01/13/trump-credit-cards-congress-republicans-00725477) on Trump’s new affordability directives. But the conservative groups’ letter aims to ensure that continues to be the case. “We encourage all members to remain steadfast in their opposition against government overreach via interest rate cap proposals and the \[Credit Card Competition Act\],” they wrote.

**NETCHOICE ASKS GOP TO DROP APP STORE BILL:** “Right-leaning tech group [**NetChoice** privately warned](https://subscriber.politicopro.com/article/2026/01/netchoice-privately-urges-house-gop-to-shelve-app-store-bill-00739255) House Energy and Commerce Committee Chair **Brett Guthrie** (R-Ky.) earlier this month that an app store age-verification bill advanced in December by an E&C subcommittee would be ‘swiftly struck down’ in court,” **POLITICO**’s **Brendan Bordelon** reports.

— “In a [memo obtained by POLITICO](https://subscriber.politicopro.com/f/?id=0000019b-e189-d0ec-a3bb-f1d987050000), dated Jan. 12 and addressed to Guthrie and others on the committee, NetChoice said the App Store Accountability Act — a GOP-led bill that would require app stores and developers to verify the age of their users and require parental consent for each purchase — ‘suffers from myriad constitutional deficiencies under the First Amendment.’”

**LIQUOR STORES TEAM UP ON HEMP:** After losing the fight last year to head off new federal hemp regulations, alcohol retailers have launched a new coalition to push for a delay in implementing the new rules.

— The **Beverage Alcohol Merchants Coalition**, whose members include **Total Wine & More**, **Gopuff** and **ABC Fine Wine & Spirits**, is backing a bill from Rep. **Jim Baird** (R-Ind.) and Sen. **Amy Klobuchar** (D-Minn.) that would delay the new regulations by two years.

— Hemp farmers and alcohol companies have argued that delaying the compliance deadline for last year’s ban on intoxicating hemp — which supporters of the ban had derided as a loophole — would provide the industry with much-needed certainty while lawmakers hash out a comprehensive regulatory framework.
