# Health lobbying bonanza continues

By [KELLY HOOPER](https://www.politico.com/staff/kelly-hooper) and [SOPHIE GARDNER](https://www.politico.com/staff/sophie-gardner)

02/18/2026 10:00 AM EST

## Driving the Day

![K Street.](https://www.politico.com/dims4/default/1e494c8/2147483647/resize/1524x/quality/90/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic.politico.com%2F84%2Fc0%2F72276f8446a79457780ff8a3a174%2Fstimulus-lobbying.JPEG)

The health industry is flooding the Capitol with lobbyists as pressure mounts to lower drug prices. \| Charles Dharapak/AP

**JOINING THE RANKS —** Health care companies are mounting their defenses in Washington with more than two dozen lobbying registrations filed since Friday as the industry comes under scrutiny for its role in the high cost of care, Amanda reports.

Leading the pack is Monument Advocacy, which registered to lobby on behalf of nine companies Friday, including pharmaceutical distribution giants Cencora and McKesson. Boutique health care lobbying shops are behind many new registrations: Jeffrey J. Kimbell & Associates, for example, registered to lobby for three companies, including medical device makers Coloplast and Rayner. And Hittle Strategies registered two new companies.

**Big picture:** The filings suggest that last year’s health care lobbying boom is far from over. Last year marked Washington’s [most lucrative year](https://subscriber.politicopro.com/article/2026/01/flush-times-have-returned-to-the-washington-swamp-00740098) for health care lobbying on record, as President **Donald Trump**’s health policy moves posed the biggest threat to industry profits in years.

“Companies both large and small are continuing to look for relationships and opportunities in Washington while also recognizing the necessity of staying abreast of the near-daily swirl of issues impacting the life sciences industry,” **Jeffrey J. Kimbell** said.

The filings also indicate a rush by companies to influence the legislative agenda after Congress just signed into law a health care package that includes long-awaited policy items like telehealth extensions and reforms to pharmacy benefit managers, which negotiate drug prices between health plans and drug companies.

**What’s next:** High on the list this year will be affordability. Players ranging from drugmakers to insurers and their PBMs are spending millions to shape the narrative around who’s responsible for high drug prices as the sector faces mounting pressure from the Trump administration and Congress to lower prices ahead of the midterm elections.

“Congress just cleared the decks. … We’re going to need to be thinking of the next list of policy changes that will help increase affordability, drive prices down,” said **Taylor Hittle,** a former Republican staffer and HHS official in the first Trump administration. Hittle has been hired to lobby for health care startup Nomi Health and the Partnership for Employer-Sponsored Coverage, an alliance of employers that advocates for PBM reform.

**Inside K Street:** The recent filings highlight how boutique firms are poised to benefit from healthcare’s lobbying bonanza and are hot targets for acquisition. Last year, Monument Advocacy merged with health care-focused shop Horizon Government Affairs, founded by **Joel White,** a former Republican staff director on the House Ways and Means Health Subcommittee. Many of its recent filings were previous clients at Horizon. A few months later, FGS Global acquired Tarplin, Downs & Young, a top lobbying shop for drugmakers.

“With the Trump administration and key congressional committees sharpening their focus ahead of the midterms, stakeholders aren’t sitting on the sidelines. Health care drives a significant share of federal spending, sits at the center of multiple legislative vehicles, and remains one of the most dynamic and heavily scrutinized sectors in Washington,” White said.

“If you don’t jump on the opportunity to do things when the timing is right politically, it just gets harder down the road,” Hittle said, citing the Republican trifecta government as a motivator.

## IN THE COURTS

![ICE agents detain a female immigrant in an immigration court hallway.         ](https://www.politico.com/dims4/default/57104da/2147483647/resize/1524x/quality/90/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fstatic.politico.com%2Fdc%2Fa0%2F171aa3d5419baba75c8bbd430de1%2Fhttps-delivery-gettyimages.com%2Fdownloads%2F2226092108)

ICE is detaining pregnant women, putting some at risk for miscarriages — and seemingly against the agency's own policy. \| Dominic Gwinn/Middle East Images/AFP via Getty Images

**JUDGES RAISE ALARM**— Federal judges are decrying the [Trump administration’s treatment of pregnant and nursing detainees](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/02/18/pregnant-nursing-ice-detainees-00784683) in Immigration and Customs Enforcement custody, POLITICO’s Kyle Cheney reports.

The judges’ warnings come as the Trump administration has given the courts conflicting, unclear answers about whether it follows its own policies that sharply restrict those detentions.

**Why it matters:** The courts are confronted with harrowing stories about women being separated from their nursing infants or housed in cramped and ill-equipped ICE facilities while pregnant, in conditions that threaten their health and have, in some cases, been followed by miscarriages.

That crystallized over the weekend in Massachusetts, when **Djeniffer Benvinda Semedo,** a Cape Verdean national who is 6 months pregnant, was [rushed to Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/27172503/5.pdf) for emergency care. The woman said that ICE held her in a temporary holding facility for three days, exacerbating her medical distress. On Tuesday, ICE released Benvinda Semedo ahead of an emergency effort by her attorneys to ask a judge for her release, though she remains hospitalized.

Last month, U.S. District Judge **Michael Davis,** a Clinton appointee based in Minnesota, [took the Trump administration to task](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/01/26/minnesota-immigration-cases-ice-00746275) for detaining and transferring to Texas a legally admitted refugee who was still breastfeeding her 5-month-old. Around the same time, U.S. District Judge **Richard Boulware,** an Obama appointee in Nevada, [ordered the immediate release](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72171641/sahin-v-rader/?order_by=desc) of a woman who said her detention by ICE — despite a high-risk pregnancy — had violated a [2021 policy](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/detention/11032.4_IdentificationMonitoringPregnantPostpartumNursingIndividuals.pdf) that bars the detention of pregnant and nursing immigrants in most cases.

**Background:** For months, ICE has declined to say whether the Biden-era policy, which allows for detention only in “exceptional circumstances” such as threats to national security, remains in force. A Justice Department attorney [told a federal judge in August](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/27172523/nelsonorder.pdf) that the policy had been effectively revoked by President **Donald Trump**’s Day One [executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/protecting-the-american-people-against-invasion/).

But another DOJ attorney [said last month the policy was still in effect](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72171641/sahin-v-rader/?order_by=desc) and binding on ICE. Last week, a federal judge pressed the Trump administration to say why the nursing mother of a 6-month-old had been detained. In response, DOJ said it consulted with the Department of Homeland Security, which [was “unable to identify”](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/27172491/govt-response-to-order-for-supplement-1.pdf) any reason to keep her detained.

POLITICO has repeatedly asked about the policy and hasn’t received clarity on whether it remains in force. Officials at DHS and ICE did not respond to a request for comment Tuesday.

**ROUNDUP SETTLEMENT PROPOSAL** **—** Agricultural chemical company Monsanto [is proposing a $7.25 billion settlement](https://subscriber.politicopro.com/article/2026/02/monsanto-plans-to-announce-a-proposal-to-settle-roundup-class-action-lawsuits-for-7-25-billion-00784256) to resolve claims that its Roundup weed killer is linked to non-Hodgkin lymphoma, POLITICO’s Marcia Brown reports.

Under the plan filed in the Circuit Court for the City of St. Louis, Missouri, where Monsanto is based, victims could file for compensation through a system that would award damages based on exposure type and the severity of their medical condition.

**Background:** The proposal, which needs court approval, follows the Supreme Court’s decision to take up a case that would limit future similar litigation against Bayer, Monsanto’s parent company. The company said in a statement that the case, _Durnell v. Monsanto_, remains “critical” to its efforts to counter unresolved litigation involving Roundup. The suit was filed against Monsanto in 2019, before Bayer acquired the company.

“This proposed settlement represents a historic step toward bringing justice and financial relief to thousands of people across the country who developed non-Hodgkin lymphoma after exposure to Roundup,” **Christopher Seeger** of Seeger Weiss LLP, one of the attorneys representing the class, said in a statement.

**Key context:** The settlement comes as the Trump administration navigates pressure from Make America Healthy Again activists pushing government officials to ban or limit certain pesticides, including glyphosate, the active ingredient in Roundup.

“This litigation and the resulting cost underscore the need for guidance from the Supreme Court on clear regulation in American agriculture,” Bayer CEO **Bill Anderson** said in a statement. The class settlement and Supreme Court case are both necessary to help bring the strongest, most certain and most timely containment to this litigation.”

## Global Health

**DEAL DEADLINE LOOMS —** The latest round of talks on a pandemic deal at the World Health Organization wrapped up Friday with little progress made and just five official negotiating days scheduled before the May deadline, POLITICO’s Rory O’Neill reports.

**Background:** Negotiators are stuck on an annex to the pandemic treaty — an agreement to protect the world from future pandemics. The annex addresses the agreement’s most controversial element, a new system to govern the sharing of pathogen samples and data, as well as the resulting drugs and vaccines needed to combat a pandemic threat.

The U.S. withdrew from the pandemic agreement talks when President **Donald Trump** took office, but Biden administration officials had been reluctant to make binding commitments to share vaccines and therapies with some countries.

**What’s next:** Negotiators need to agree to “lay out the basic principles by May” and continue further, more detailed work afterwards, a European Union official, granted anonymity to discuss the negotiations freely, told POLITICO. Two other negotiators confirmed they had heard that suggestion, too, signaling a growing view among diplomats that the May deadline is too ambitious given the progress made so far.

The deadline was set [in the resolution adopted by countries at the World Health Assembly last year](https://subscriber.politicopro.com/article/2025/05/countries-adopt-who-pandemic-agreement-00358324).

**Key context:** The [latest draft](https://api.politico.eu/editorial_documents/662072f6-a7ee-44e9-ac02-8f23b5c88d62), circulated Friday and obtained by POLITICO, shows that officials have much left to agree on. That’s likely because the two sides — roughly divided between high-income and developing countries — have different priorities.

The former wants guaranteed, low-friction access to pathogen samples so researchers and companies can quickly develop drugs and vaccines. Developing countries fear a repeat of the Covid-19 pandemic, when they were left waiting at the back of the line for drugs and vaccines. They want access to samples and data to be conditional on guaranteeing access to life-saving technology.

**What’s next:** Negotiators will meet again in Geneva from March 23 to 28.
